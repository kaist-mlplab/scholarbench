ScholarBench: A Bilingual Benchmark for Abstraction, Comprehension, and Reasoning Evaluation in Academic Contexts

An English–Korean benchmark of academic-paper questions across eight research domains

Findings of EMNLP 2025

Dongwon Noh1,*, Donghyeok Koh1,*, Junghun Yuk2,*, Gyuwan Kim3,*, Jae Yong Lee4, KyungTae Lim2,†, Cheoneum Park1,†

1HBNU 2KAIST 3UCSB 4KISTI

*Equal contribution   †Corresponding authors

Paper (ACL Anthology) PDF arXiv Code

Abstract

Prior benchmarks for evaluating the domain-specific knowledge of large language models (LLMs) lack the scalability to handle complex academic tasks. To address this, we introduce ScholarBench, a benchmark centered on deep expert knowledge and complex academic problem-solving, which evaluates the academic reasoning ability of LLMs and is constructed through a three-step process. ScholarBench targets more specialized and logically complex contexts derived from academic literature, encompassing five distinct problem types. Unlike prior benchmarks, ScholarBench evaluates the abstraction, comprehension, and reasoning capabilities of LLMs across eight distinct research domains. To ensure high-quality evaluation data, we define category-specific example attributes and design questions that are aligned with the characteristic research methodologies and discourse structures of each domain. Additionally, this benchmark operates as an English-Korean bilingual dataset, facilitating simultaneous evaluation for linguistic capabilities of LLMs in both languages. The benchmark comprises 5,031 examples in Korean and 5,309 in English, with even state-of-the-art models like o3-mini achieving an average evaluation score of only 0.543, demonstrating the challenging nature of this benchmark.

Keywords Academic benchmark Scholarly QA LLM evaluation Domain-specific knowledge Bilingual (English–Korean) Korean LLM benchmark Academic reasoning Summarization Reading comprehension

Benchmark Construction

ScholarBench tests three abilities: abstraction (summarizing an academic paragraph), reasoning (answering from parametric knowledge in a closed-book setting) and comprehension (answering when the source paragraph is given, open-book). Every paragraph yields five question types: summarization, short answer, multiple choice, multiple selection, and true/false.

  1. Task design and collection. Eight academic categories with 63 English and 65 Korean attributes are defined. English sources are 200 articles from 80 open-access journals ranked in the top 10 by Google Scholar H5-index; Korean sources are 1,916 articles from 138 journals selected by KCI citation index.
  2. Question generation. GPT-4o generates the five question types from each source paragraph.
  3. Refinement and review. Two sampled outputs are used as few-shot examples to refine generation, then 15 reviewers (11 English, 4 Korean) check the paragraph, verify each answer, and rate quality. Inter-annotator Kappa is 0.621 for English and 0.742 for Korean.

Summarization is scored with ROUGE-1/2/L, short answers with BERTScore, and multiple-choice, multiple-selection (2, 3 or 4 correct options: A-2, A-3, A-4) and true/false questions with accuracy.

Main Results

  • Size. 5,309 English and 5,031 Korean examples across eight categories and five question types; 18.7% of the questions are parallel English–Korean pairs.
  • Hard for current LLMs. In the closed-book setting the best average score is 0.543 (o3-mini), followed by GPT-4o (0.534), Llama-70b (0.530) and Qwen-72b (0.529).
  • No single best model. GPT-4o leads ROUGE-1 (0.417), Mistral-24b leads ROUGE-2/L (0.159 / 0.359), Qwen-72b leads MCQ (0.755) and true/false (0.811), and Qwen-32b-reasoning leads three-answer multiple selection (0.640).
  • Context helps. With the source paragraph provided (open-book), average scores rise, e.g. GPT-4o from 0.534 to 0.629 and o3-mini from 0.543 to 0.609; Qwen-72b reaches 0.900 MCQ accuracy.
  • Bilingual gap. On the parallel subset, most models score higher in Korean than in English, while some global models show the opposite pattern.
